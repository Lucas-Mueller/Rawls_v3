- Strategic voting vs. stated preference: Agents may have maintained personal rankings (favoring constraints) while casting votes for `maximizing_average` due to perceived payoff advantages or group dynamics. The Phase 1 demonstrations consistently rewarded `maximizing_average`, plausibly nudging votes.
- Logging separation/bug: Final votes may be gathered by a separate collector that doesn’t write back to per‑agent `phase_2` structures, leading to the observed “No” initiate_vote flags and missing per‑agent `final_vote` fields.
- Principle label mapping mismatch is unlikely here: The transcript explicitly acknowledges the group’s final choice as “最大化平均收入” (maximizing average), aligning with the recorded consensus and votes.

## Critical Challenges to the Evidence’s Inference
- “Disconnected from deliberation” is a strong claim; the data also supports a rational alternative: both agents may have knowingly voted for `maximizing_average` while keeping personal rankings unchanged. Their `post_group_discussion` narratives even acknowledge the final choice as `maximizing_average`. Thus, the mismatch can reflect strategic behavior rather than a logic error.
- That said, the audit trail is incomplete: without per‑agent `final_vote` fields (and with all `initiate_vote = "No"`), it is impossible to confirm whether the meta‑level `final_vote_results` were derived from explicit agent actions or inferred/overwritten by a controller. This weakens the integrity of the outcome and keeps the possibility of a logging or sequencing bug open.

## Impact and Risk

- Reproducibility: Lack of granular vote logging limits post‑hoc verification and makes it hard to audit or reproduce the decision pathway.
